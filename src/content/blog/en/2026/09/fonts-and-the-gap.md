## The Idea

The page was supposed to be simple.

Choose a font.
Place the text.
Publish the page.

At first glance, several fonts seemed perfectly reasonable.

They looked elegant.

They looked readable.

They looked finished.

## What Happened

The problem appeared only after real content was added.

A typeface that looked beautiful in isolation suddenly felt decorative once actual writing was placed inside it.

The issue wasn't the font itself.

The issue appeared between the font and the text.

And then between the text and the reader.

The same letters that looked right on a sample page felt different when they carried real conversations.

The gap only became visible after the page existed.

## What Changed

The discussion gradually shifted away from the question of which font looked best.

Instead, the question became:

Which font disappears?

A constellation page should not ask readers to admire typography.

It should allow them to follow ideas.

Several options were tested, adjusted, rejected, and revisited.

No single decision solved the problem.

Understanding came from seeing the page repeatedly under real conditions.

## Result

The final choice was not the font that looked most impressive.

It was the font that allowed the content to breathe.

The lesson extended beyond typography.

Many parts of this project have followed the same pattern.

Something can look right when viewed alone.

Only after it becomes part of a larger system do we discover whether it actually belongs there.

The page became a reminder that design is not made of isolated pieces.

It emerges from the relationship between them.
